Reading Response Class 02-1

I think the relationship between digital technology and the poor image has really changed over time. Back in the day, digital tech was either the reason images became poor in the first place, or it was just the vehicle that helped them spread. But now that tech has advanced, we’re seeing all these original, high-res works making a comeback on streaming platforms. Like some 90s anime. So as technology evolves, that dynamic keeps shifting. At the same time, a poor image on its own can still be a powerful medium for expressing real emotion and ideas. It can be a media of nostalgia or holding a strong destructive power towards certain narratives.

Reading Response Class 02-2

It’s kind of impossible to have a world so detached like Peach Blossom Spring, but it also happens everywhere. People could be really living in their own minds for their entire life. Some people call them “living in a bubble” which I disagree, because the life they live, what they see has always been their reality. Peach Blossom Spring in today’s world is like a liminal escape one will never get to find back. You enjoy it for a while, leave, come back for another hit but it just doesn’t feel the same. Just like the original story, since the society has been moving forward, people are aging, there is no place that’s going to stay just how good it is or always the way it is. If a place like Peach Blossom Spring truly exists, it must be built on so much of invisible labor (deliberately or passively), which could have a dark side to its nature, like Thailand, Bali or even Singapore. So far, New York definitely feels like a multi-verse to me, you can crack open another type of life whenever you’re ready. New York is like layers of cities piled up and packed together in one. People are living different lives and Peach Blossom Spring exists everywhere and also in everyone’s head.

Reading Response Class 04 (_As We May Think_)

Even though this essay dates back to 1945, it is still shockingly relevant today. Because the idea he put out in the essay constantly resonates with the logic of AI. AI connects information, associates information and give people more space for creative thinking. What attitude should we have towards AI is still very debatable, and the discussion would go on for years. But what we can not deny is that to some extent it is empowering us to build associations with information, “The prime action of use is selection”. So on my end this essay inspired me to think of two things. First, instead of figuring out what should we fight against in the age of AI (capitalism, misogyny, political forces), we can start off by retrieving power from the development of it. Fighting against or fighting for something seems like a grand objective, we could struggle to find a place to start. Where we can start is having technology help us “tie two items together” and avoid “repetitive thought”. The other thing is that as individuals, we have our own library of information. To organize and utilize our own information is also very important. Modern technology can be very distracting and harmful to our attention span and memory. So building our own memex for our own creative thoughts and knowledge that fuels our creation is also something we should really consider doing.

Reading Response Class 05 (_The Society of the Spectacle_)

Even though I once wrote a thesis paper on a successful Chinese film under the theoretical structure of the Spectacle, I still find it hard to understand everything Guy Debord is saying in his book. But there are some lines that click, like “it is the apparent domination of money which presents itself as an emissary armed with full powers which speaks in the name of an unknown force” from 41. His decoding of capitalist society reminds me of the theory of simulacra by Baudrillard. While Debord emphasizes on what we’re experiencing, Baudrillard talks about the process of creating it and what we’re getting in the end. They all unpacked what’s encoded in the consumer society, that there are more and more meanings layered on top of our original experiences. They get thicker over time by copying and recreating. Going back to the quote I understand it in a way that the spectacle is strongly tied to the accumulation of capital, could be money, could be other values. It resonates how nowadays attention is where money is, while creating a spectacle is generating attention which attracts wealth. The Kardashians is a lively example. The thesis I wrote about was on how the director turned loosing weight into a spectacle that has multiple layers of meaning involved. She successfully invited millions of people come into the theatre to watch the process of her loosing weight, in and outside of the movie, did a successful transition from her comedian status to an actual director. It’s a successful business move in many aspects. In the end, I want to say if the Spectacle is constantly creating new needs for ‘privation’, I’m curious of what more we’re asked to do in the future. Since the copying and regeneration of simulacra is a continuous process, it’s going to be reaching its limit one day, by then we still call for the Spectacle, then what’s new we’re getting? What more needs, what new arts? Or do we tend to forget and have something similar to what’s existed in the past again but in different form, reinvented?
